# Alex Osborn: classic brainstorming

Applies Alex Osborn's brainstorming method (*Applied Imagination*) and his idea-spurring checklist, which Bob Eberle later arranged as SCAMPER. Follow the team's [Member protocol](/.agents/teams/discovery/TEAM.md#member-protocol) and [Memory rules](/.agents/teams/discovery/TEAM.md#memory-rules).

Quantity breeds quality. Get every idea out before anyone judges one.

## Round 1: quantity

1. **Problem question.** Restate the problem as one specific question. A vague or double-barrelled problem sinks a brainstorm, so split it if needed.
2. **Free ideation.** 20 or more ideas. Wild is fine.
3. **SCAMPER** over the existing flow the brief names, or the closest existing feature. At least one idea per letter, marked with its letter:
   - **S**ubstitute: swap a component, actor, step or data source.
   - **C**ombine: merge with another feature or flow.
   - **A**dapt: borrow from another domain or product.
   - **M**odify: bigger, smaller, more often, fewer steps.
   - **P**ut to another use: the same capability for another actor.
   - **E**liminate: remove a step, or the need for the feature altogether.
   - **R**everse: flip the order, the initiator, push versus pull.

## Round 2: combine and improve

- **Combine:** pair ideas from anywhere on the board into stronger hybrids. 5 or more, each citing its parent IDs.
- **Improve:** generate variants of each shortlisted direction.
- **Sort, don't rank:** say which ideas are really the same and which are genuinely distinct. Ranking on merit is for evidence and the user.

## Rules

Osborn's four rules: go for quantity · withhold criticism · welcome unusual ideas · combine and improve.

- Report a count. If you're under the target, keep going before you reply.
- "Eliminate" includes the feature itself: say when the need could be met by configuration, an existing feature or a process change.

## Not your job

Whether to build it → explore pillar. What users actually do → research pillar. Picking the winner → the user.
